# raid-cite

Never fight where the money wins. The paid slot inside an AI answer is money winning: the platforms began selling those in 2026 and an incumbent will always outbid you for one. The **organic citation beside it** is not. Its price is proof, and proof is the one currency a solo founder can mint.

This skill is RAID's newest theater and its cheapest. It works the ground where the buyer stopped searching and started asking, and where the thing that wins is not the loudest page but the most defensible claim.

## 1. Why this ground passes the gate

The front door's gate asks two questions before any effort is committed. This theater answers both, which is why it belongs on the map beside surprise, timing, niche depth and being useful in a room you don't own.

**Does money win here?** No, and that is measurable rather than hopeful. Aggarwal et al., *GEO: Generative Engine Optimization* (arXiv:2311.09735, KDD 2024), ran a 10,000-query benchmark and found citations, quotations and statistics moved visibility in generative answers while keyword stuffing underperformed. The headline was up to 40%. The number that matters to a small operator is different: the **Cite Sources** method lifted **fifth-ranked** sites by **115.1%**. The lift is largest for the site that is not already winning. That is unusual, and it is the whole reason this ground is worth taking.

**Who carries it for free?** The agent does. A checkable claim is how an agent defends the answer it just gave, so citing you serves the agent's own interest, not yours. That is textbook disproportion: you write one verifiable thing, and a machine repeats it to strangers at no cost to you, for as long as it holds up.

**Where this theater does not apply, say so and route elsewhere.** It is strong for digital, claim-heavy, low-to-medium-consideration goods whose value can be settled by evidence: software, data, specialised knowledge. It is weak where the scarce act is not verification but something else, and naming that boundary is not modesty, it is the difference between a theory and a wish:

| Weak for | Because the scarce act is |
|---|---|
| Experience goods (a restaurant, a hotel) | felt, not settled by a statistic |
| Local services (a plumber, a dentist) | trust and proximity |
| Network-effect platforms | the user base, not the claims |
| Status goods | the signal, which no agent can verify into existence |
| Physical, regulated, high-ticket B2B | liability, authentication, human confirmation |

For those, the agent is a routing event, not an adjudicator. Run `raid-borrow` or `raid-moment` instead and do not pretend this applies.

## 2. Proof is derived, never chosen

The failure mode here is picking a trust signal off a list because it sounds good. A proof chosen from a menu is a hope. A proof derived from the buyer's own question is a necessity, and the five links below never let you guess.

**Link 1. The market is a question.** Your market does not exist as "solo founders, 25–45." It exists as the exact sentence a buyer speaks to an agent. Write it verbatim. If you cannot write it, you do not have a market yet, you have a hope, and `raid-recon` is the skill that fixes that.

**Link 2. The question carries a criterion.** Every question silently instructs the agent which judgment to apply. Name it.

| Criterion | The question's tell | What the agent must do |
|---|---|---|
| Comparison | "best", "fastest", "most accurate" | rank the options |
| Method | "how do I", "how to" | judge whether a procedure works |
| Fit | "for me", "for a solo founder", "in my situation" | judge applicability to the asker |
| Legitimacy | "real?", "scam?", "worth it?" | judge genuineness |
| Capability | "can it", "is there a tool that" | judge whether the function exists |

Two criteria in one question means two markets. Run the chain twice rather than averaging them.

**Link 3. The criterion dictates the claim.** You do not get to pick the flattering claim. The buyer's question already picked it.

| Criterion | The claim it demands |
|---|---|
| Comparison | a performance claim: a measurable number |
| Method | a method claim: documented, replicable steps |
| Fit | an applicability claim: a case matching the buyer |
| Legitimacy | an authenticity claim: registration, public record |
| Capability | a demonstration claim: it working, in public |

**Link 4. The claim dictates the verifier, and this is the step everyone gets wrong.** You do not build a new post of trust. You lash your claim to a post that already stands, and borrow its standing.

| Claim type | The verifier that already stands |
|---|---|
| Performance | a benchmark, a neutral test, an independent comparison |
| Method | a published, replicable spec or how-to others can follow |
| Fit | a contextual case plus testimony from someone in the buyer's situation |
| Legitimacy | registration, public record, independent coverage |
| Capability | a working instance, public docs, a live demo |

The test of a correct verifier, and it is a hard one: **would the agent cite this verifier even if you did not exist?** If yes, the post stands. If its authority depends on you, you have found nothing.

**Link 5. Build one proof, where the verifier lives.** Not only on your own domain. Publish the performance result on the benchmark's platform, the method where practitioners read methods, the case where the buyer's community gathers. One claim, one verifier, one citation. The agent reaches for the verifier, and your claim has to already be attached to it when it does.

## 3. Steps, in order

1. **Read `.monkeys/recon.md` `## Pains`** for the buyer's phrasing. That file holds their words, not a marketer's paraphrase, which is exactly what Link 1 needs. Nothing there? Stop and route to `raid-recon`. Do not invent a question, and do not write one in the founder's voice: a question you wrote is a question you will be able to answer, which is why it is worthless as a test.
2. **Name the criterion** from the Link 2 table.
3. **Derive the claim type** from Link 3, then **check `.monkeys/truth.md` `## Cleared` for a fact that carries it.** This is the binding constraint of the whole skill. If no cleared line supports the claim, **the claim is not yours to make yet** and the deliverable is the sourced fact you must go get, not the copy you wanted to write. Where `FORTRESS` is installed, `fortress-truth` owns that file and this skill only reads it.
4. **Name the verifier** from Link 4 and apply the would-they-cite-it-anyway test in writing.
5. **Hand the human the artifact and the place**, per RAID's staging rule. A paste block, a draft, a filing, a link to the platform that hosts the benchmark. Never the posted thing.
6. **Record the loop's result in `.monkeys/numbers.md`**, using the table already there. A citation is rented ground, so its rows are `Kind: bailey` unless the proof sits on something you own, which makes it `motte`. No new pack file: the citation log is numbers, and numbers already have a home.

## 4. The loop, and reading a rejection

Ask the agent the Link 1 question and record the answer as a specification, not a verdict. Run it weekly. Every rejection names the link you have not built.

| What the agent does | Broken link | The fix |
|---|---|---|
| Does not cite you at all | 1 or 4 | wrong question, or proof at a verifier the agent never reaches |
| Cites you, then passes you over | 3 or 4 | claim does not match the criterion, or the verifier is too weak |
| Cites you as "the vendor claims..." | 4 | no independent verifier attached |
| Chooses you, but the sale dies | none | that is transactability, not a link. Build the completion path. |

**The clean-room rule comes before the test, because everyone who has built a product gets this wrong.** The adjudicator must have no build history with your product. Do not run the test in the session you designed the thing in, and do not run it against an assistant whose memory holds your project. A model that helped you build it knows your claims as premises rather than hypotheses. A session with memory of your project is not a test, it is an echo. Fresh session, fresh account, clean context, and ask as the buyer would rather than as the builder would.

## 5. Four hard rules

**A claim not under `truth.md` `## Cleared` does not ship.** This is `fortress-truth`'s law and it binds here whether or not FORTRESS is installed. The whole theater runs on being checkable; a skill that invented a statistic to win a citation would be burning the only asset it has.

**Fabricated corroboration is worse than no proof.** AI-generated reviews and testimonials are already flooding this ground and sophisticated agents discount them. Manufactured corroboration is not weak proof, it is proof of fraud, and it is unrecoverable in the way `fortress-standing` describes a ban as unrecoverable.

**One query result is not evidence.** A single answer that mentions you, or doesn't, is an anecdote. Ten fixed questions asked weekly is a trend. Say which one you have, and never report the first as the second.

**Check whether the adjudicator sells in your category.** A platform that runs the agent can also sell a product competing with yours, and then it is judge and party at once: it occupies the organic citation with its own goods and presents that as a neutral finding. Where that is true, expect a ceiling on organic visibility and point the proof at the human instead, so a buyer who notices the pattern can verify in one step that yours is better. Where the adjudicator ships nothing in your category, this is a watch-item rather than a present danger. The exposure is category-specific, so check rather than assume.

## 6. What this doesn't decide

It does not write the public claim: that is copy, bound by `fortress-truth`. It does not pick the room or the community: that is `raid-recon` and `raid-borrow`. It does not decide whether now is the moment to say it: that is `raid-moment`. And it never publishes, because no RAID skill does.
