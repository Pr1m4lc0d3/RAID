# raid-multiply

Never fight where the money wins. Producing many separate pieces of content from scratch costs time you don't have; cutting many pieces out of one substantial piece costs a fraction of that. One asset, twenty cuts — the literal time-instead-of-money trade.

## 1. What one piece yields

A single substantial piece — a long article, a deep demo, a real conversation, a detailed guide — can yield, at minimum:

- the piece itself
- a thread that walks through its core argument
- a short video built from its strongest moment
- a set of standalone quotes
- a diagram of its central idea
- an email that leads with its hook
- an answer, ready to paste, for the room where this question comes up (see `raid-borrow`)
- a reference you can keep linking for a year

That's eight without straining. Twenty is a target for how thoroughly to mine the source, not a quota to pad with filler cuts that don't earn their place.

## 2. Make first, then cut

Make the substantial thing first. Cutting is cheap; making is not, and trying to make twenty pieces natively instead of one deep piece plus nineteen cuts is the expensive path this skill exists to avoid. The order matters: a rushed source piece produces twenty cuts of something thin, and cutting doesn't add substance that wasn't in the source.

## 3. Every cut stands alone

A cut that only makes sense to someone who already saw the original is not a cut — it's a fragment, and a fragment doesn't work as a standalone post because it assumes context the reader doesn't have. Each cut needs its own frame: enough setup that it lands on its own, even for someone who will never see the source piece. If a quote needs "as I said earlier" to make sense, it isn't cut yet — go back and give it its own opening.

## 4. A cut inherits everything the source carried

Licence, rights, and consent all travel with the material into every cut. Cutting resets none of them, and twenty cuts is twenty uses, not one — whatever was borderline in the source is now borderline twenty times, on twenty surfaces, each one findable on its own.

Check both of these before cutting, not after:

- **The licence covers the new use and the new format.** A music bed cleared for one long video is not automatically cleared for twenty short ones, for a different platform, or for anything that runs as an ad. Footage, images and fonts carry the same limits. **A permissive-sounding label is not clearance** — "royalty-free" describes how you pay, not what you may do, and "free to use" almost always has conditions sitting under it. Read the actual terms, or don't cut that material.
- **Where a person appears or speaks, confirm they agreed to *this* use.** A guest consented to the conversation they sat down for. That is not consent to twenty standalone clips, not consent to their least-guarded remark becoming a whole post, and not consent to being the face of promotional material. Ask again for the new use. A line that passes unremarked inside an hour-long piece *is the entire message* when it is the cut.

Where either is unclear, the cut waits. Publishing on an assumed right multiplies the exposure by the number of cuts, on material you already knew you hadn't checked.

## 5. Capability note

This skill decides which cuts to make and produces the scripts and shot lists for each one. Rendering — turning a shot list into a finished video, a diagram sketch into a finished image — is the last mile, and it requires no particular tool. "Here are your twenty cuts, render them however you render things" is a complete deliverable on its own. Don't hold the cuts back waiting on a renderer, and don't name a specific rendering tool as required to use this skill.

## 6. What this doesn't decide

`raid-multiply` decides the cuts and drafts them. Where each cut gets posted is `raid-borrow`'s question if it's going into someone else's room, and whether a cut's claims are sourced is `fortress-truth`'s job where present.
