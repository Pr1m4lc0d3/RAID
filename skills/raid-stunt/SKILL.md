# raid-stunt

Never fight where the money wins. A budget buys reach directly; a stunt buys reach by getting someone else to carry it for free. If nobody carries it, you didn't run a stunt — you spent effort on a stand-in for an ad, without the ad's reach.

## 1. The carry test

Before building anything, ask: **if this works, who repeats it, and why is repeating it in their interest?**

Name the person. Not "people will share this" — which person, in which room, gets something out of repeating it? A discount for referring, a laugh worth passing on, a genuinely useful tool they'd recommend anyway, proof of a claim they wanted proof of. If you can't name the person and the reason, the honest answer is nobody, and the honest label for what you're about to build is a chore, not a stunt.

The strongest stunts pass this test structurally: **only you can run them**, because the doing of the stunt *is* a demonstration of the thing you sell. A speed claim proven by racing a slow incumbent live. A durability claim proven by handing the product to strangers to abuse. If a competitor copies a stunt like that, the copy still points at you — they're now demonstrating your category, on your terms.

## 2. Failure modes

Reject a stunt design if it matches any of these:

- **It insults the audience it needs.** A stunt that makes fun of the people you're trying to reach doesn't get carried — it gets reported. The line between "cheeky" and "insulting" is drawn by the room's own norms (see `raid-recon`), not by whether it made your team laugh.
- **It would embarrass you if it worked.** If going viral is the failure case — because the thing you'd be known for isn't the thing you're selling — don't build it on a bet that it flops quietly instead.
- **It requires a budget to be noticed.** Paid boosting, paid seeding, paying for placement to make the stunt look organic — that's an ad wearing a stunt's clothes, and money wins there. If the plan only works with a media buy behind it, it isn't a stunt.

## 3. Claim discipline still binds a stunt

The loudest play is the one most likely to be checked, screenshotted, and quoted back. A stunt built on a number nobody verified, or a comparison nobody sourced, doesn't just risk being wrong — it risks being wrong in front of the largest audience the play will ever get. Where `FORTRESS` is installed, `fortress-truth`'s register binds every claim inside a stunt exactly as it binds ordinary copy; there is no exemption for something that worked. Where it isn't installed, source it anyway — the exposure is the same either way.

## 4. What this doesn't decide

`raid-stunt` designs the play and filters it through the carry test. Whether now is the right moment to run it is `raid-moment`. Whether a claim inside it is sourced is `fortress-truth` where present, and plain honesty where it isn't.
