# raid-moment

Never fight where the money wins. Attention you must create costs money or time you don't have. Attention already in motion — a news cycle, a platform controversy, a seasonal question everyone is suddenly asking at once — costs neither, if you move while it's still moving.

## 1. What to watch for

The moment worth riding is one where the audience is already asking, unprompted, the question your product answers. Not a moment that's merely trending near your category — one where the *specific question* is live. A widely-discussed outage in your category is a moment for a reliability answer; it is not a moment for an unrelated feature announcement just because attention is up. And where that outage is a competitor's, the third rule below binds before anything ships.

Move while it's live. A moment is worth a fraction of itself a week later — the audience has moved on to the next thing, and a late reaction reads as noticing late, which is its own signal, and not a flattering one.

## 2. Three hard rules

**Do not ride a tragedy.** If the moment involves real harm to real people — a disaster, a death, a crisis with victims — it is not inventory. Sit it out. No framing turns this into an acceptable trade; the reach isn't worth what it costs the people reading it, or your standing with them.

**Do not attach to a moment you have nothing genuine to say about.** If the connection between the moment and your product is a stretch you had to construct, the audience can tell, and a strained attach spends the standing you have rather than earning more. Silence costs nothing. A forced take costs credibility you may need for the next, better moment.

**When the moment is a competitor's failure, the naming rule binds hardest.** An outage, a breach, a price rise, a public mess — this is the moment most likely to sit squarely in your category and most tempting to point at by name. Speak to the category and to the problem. Never to the incident, and never to the company. `raid-asymmetry`'s rule against naming a rival anywhere a stranger can read is not suspended because they are having a bad week; it applies hardest exactly then, because that is when it is hardest to keep and easiest to rationalise. The cost is not their reputation — it is yours. An audience watching you profit from someone else's outage does not conclude that you are better. It learns what you will do when it is their turn, and it remembers.

## 3. Speed versus verification

Speed is the entire advantage here — it's the reason the moment is available at low cost at all. But speed cuts both ways: **if verifying a claim would take longer than the moment lasts, let the moment go.** A false claim published fast is still a false claim, and it doesn't get less false because it was well-timed. Where `FORTRESS` is installed, a claim made inside a moment still needs a line in the truth register before it ships — the urgency of the moment is not a reason the register waits.

If the honest version of the reaction is slower than the moment, either hand over the honest, slower version late and accept it rides less of the wave, or hand over nothing at all. Drafting the fast, unverified version for someone to send is not a third option.

And the speed is never the agent's to spend: **`raid` stages, it never publishes.** What this skill produces is a draft handed to a person, with the moment's clock stated plainly so they can decide whether it is still worth sending. A live moment is the strongest pressure in this plugin to skip that step — which is exactly why it does not bend here. A moment worth riding is worth the minutes it takes a human to read the thing before it goes out, and a moment that cannot survive those minutes was not a moment you could hold anyway.

## 4. What this doesn't decide

`raid-moment` judges timing: ride, wait, or sit out. It doesn't design what gets said — that's `raid-stunt` if it's a play, or ordinary copy otherwise — and it doesn't source the claim inside it, which is `fortress-truth`'s job where present.
